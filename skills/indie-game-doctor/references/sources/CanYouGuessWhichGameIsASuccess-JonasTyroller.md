### 1. Market Distribution & Developer Heuristics
* **The Brutal Baseline (The Power Law):** The overwhelming majority of games released on Steam receive between 0 and 10 reviews. Review count serves as a direct proxy for sales volume and commercial success.
* **The "Developer Blindness" Trap:** Because the vast majority of games fail commercially, a developer’s intuition becomes conditioned to expect failure (guessing low numbers). Consequently, recognizing **positive outliers** (games that break out into hundreds or thousands of reviews) is the single hardest skill when evaluating game viability.
* **Effort Does Not Equal Success:** You can clearly observe extensive technical and artistic effort invested into a project (e.g., full 3D physics, complex rigging, custom assets), yet the game can still launch to under 10 reviews if key market and polish criteria are missing.

---

### 2. Trailer Direction & Presentation Rules
* **Hook Immediately with Action (No Wasted Seconds):**
  * Over 90% of low-performing game trailers start with slow studio logos, narrative fade-ins, or static text that force viewers to skip ahead.
  * A high-converting trailer starts showing responsive gameplay and action **immediately from second zero** (e.g., *Evil Awaits*).
* **Trailer Framerate & Capture Quality (Major Red Flags):**
  * Any lag, stutter, or low frame rate in the trailer footage is an immediate red flag that destroys buyer confidence (e.g., *Galactic Protection Squad*).
  * Leaving screen-recording software watermarks (such as CamStudio in *Goblin Defenders*) or unpolished capture UI permanently signals low-effort shovelware.
* **The Danger of Missing Trailer Audio:**
  * Releasing trailers without sound effects—relying only on a background music track—makes the game feel suspicious, lifeless, and incomplete (noted in *Glimmer in Mirror* and *AMazing TD*).
* **Dynamic Audio & Sound Design:**
  * High-quality sound design, punchy audio feedback, and voice acting substantially raise perceived production value (e.g., *In Other Waters*).

---

### 3. Game Feel, "Juiciness" & Visual Clarity
* **The "Juiciness" Factor (Impact & Polish):**
  * Modern indie games are judged on their "juice"—the weight of movement, screen shake, hit pauses, particles, and tactile feedback.
  * Stiff character animations or weak sound effects (e.g., weapon audio sounding like a "pea shooter" in *Dumbass Survivors*) immediately lower a game’s market reception to the 0–10 review tier.
* **Visual Clarity vs. Visual Clutter:**
  * Having many visual assets is counterproductive if the screen is overly saturated, noisy, and difficult to parse (e.g., *Louis Charles In The Louvre*).
  * Cohesive pixel sizes, clean color palettes, and legible UI are mandatory for positive reception.
* **Immediate Gameplay Comprehension:**
  * If a prospective buyer cannot understand what the core gameplay loop is within 5 seconds of looking at screenshots or video (e.g., mechanics that look "too alien" like *ROWROW*), conversion drops severely.

---

### 4. Genre Saturation & Quality Thresholds
* **The Raised Bar of Saturated Genres (2D Platformers & Metroidvanias):**
  * In highly competitive genres like 2D platformers and Metroidvanias (e.g., *Cosmic: A Journey Among Shadows*, *CardyHeim*), being "competent" or "good" is no longer enough to succeed.
  * A game is judged against genre-defining masterpieces (*Ori and the Blind Forest*, *Hollow Knight*). If it does not match that tier of excellence or offer a radically distinct hook, it will struggle to break double-digit reviews.
* **The "Survivor-Like" / Clone Saturation:**
  * Following a trend (like *Vampire Survivors*) after the market is flooded requires superior juice, audio impact, and distinctive flavor. Standard clones with generic assets inevitably stagnate at single-digit reviews.
* **Obvious Mobile Ports on Steam:**
  * Retaining mobile-oriented UI layouts, portrait/square aspect ratios, and simple touch-centric mechanics (e.g., *Knight Solitaire*) translates into immediate rejection by the core PC Steam audience.

---

### 5. Pricing Strategy vs. Perceived Value
* **Overpricing Without Matching Polish:**
  * Charging mid-tier indie prices (€15 to €17) for games that have budget or unrefined aesthetics creates immense purchasing friction (e.g., *Evil Awaits*, *Cosmic*, *BLANK SPACE*).
  * Even if a game has extensive features, overpricing relative to visual fidelity suppresses sales into the double digits.
* **Simple Mechanics at Premium Casual Prices:**
  * Charging €9 to €10 for standard casual puzzle games or basic brick-breakers (e.g., *Kubikon 3D*, *Dungeonoid 2*) fails because comparable experiences exist for free or under €2.
* **Free-to-Play as a Volume Driver:**
  * Releasing as Free-to-Play drastically lowers friction and raises the baseline review count (e.g., *Cat Warfare*, *Maru Expedition*, *AMazing TD*), turning zero-review games into triple-digit review titles provided the gameplay is interactive or multiplayer.

---

### 6. Social Proof, Publishing & Credibility Signals
* **The "Publisher Floor":**
  * Having a recognized publisher almost always establishes a minimum review baseline (rarely 0). Publisher staff, existing community networks, and marketing distribution push review counts past the initial zero-visibility hurdle.
* **Press Endorsements & Awards in Media:**
  * Legitimate accolades (e.g., IndieCade winners) and review quotes from established publications (*Eurogamer*, *Rock Paper Shotgun*, *IGN*, *GameSpot*, *Destructoid*) embedded directly into store assets provide massive credibility that correlates with thousands of reviews (e.g., *In Other Waters*, *Guacamelee!*).
* **Franchise & Naming Cues:**
  * Subtitles indicating sequels or enhanced versions (e.g., "Gold Edition", numbered entries like *Monster Prom 4*) signal that a prior iteration was profitable enough to justify further development, indicating strong built-in audience loyalty.
* **Abandoned Early Access Red Flags:**
  * An Early Access game that has not received developer updates for over a year (e.g., 18 months in *Wanderer's Shade*) communicates abandonment, completely stalling incoming sales.

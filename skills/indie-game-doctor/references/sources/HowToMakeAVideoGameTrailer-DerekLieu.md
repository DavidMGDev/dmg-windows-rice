*Derived from Unity’s Masterclass: "How to Make a Video Game Trailer & Communicate Game Vision" featuring veteran Game Trailer Director Derek Lieu & Community Manager Jackson Stevens.*

## 1. Core Philosophy: Product Vision, Design & Marketing Alignment

### 1.1. The Interconnection of Design & Pitch
* **The Marketing Decision IS the Game Design Decision:** As game marketing expert Chris Zukowski notes, the single most important marketing decision made is **what game you choose to build**. If the core fantasy cannot be communicated clearly in visual/interactive terms, the game will struggle to find an audience.
* **The "Lucas Pope" Test:** During the early prototyping phase of *Papers, Please*, Lucas Pope conceptualized what the trailer and gameplay loop would look like. If a compelling, clear trailer cannot be visualized early on, reconsider the concept.
* **The Stand-Up Comedian Mindset:** Treat your game presentation like an unknown stand-up comedian stepping onto an open-mic stage. 
  * You have **3 to 5 seconds** before the audience tunes out.
  * Do not spend time introducing yourself, showing developer logos, or explaining backstories.
  * **Deliver the punchline/gameplay hook immediately.**

### 2.1. Trailer Scope for Indies vs. AAA
* **The Minimum Viable Strategy:** Most indie teams only need **1 to 3 well-crafted trailers** (e.g., Announce, Gameplay/Demo, Launch).
* **Avoid Artificial "Marketing Beats":** Never create a trailer just to "remind people the game exists." If you don't have a major event, festival (e.g., Wholesome Direct, Summer Game Fest, Steam Next Fest), or significant milestone (Console port, Demo, Release Date), creating redundant trailers dilutes interest and makes the game look repetitive.
* **Target Multiple Audiences Simultaneously:** A trailer is not just for players browsing Steam; it is a business card for **streamers, press, platform holders (Valve, Nintendo, Sony, Xbox), and publishers**.

## 3. Structural Directing: The 5-Part Trailer Anatomy

Derek Lieu outlines the fundamental 5-stage intensity curve for game trailers:

```
Intensity
   ▲
   │        (Intrigue)                                   (Climax)
   │        ┌───────┐                                    ┌───────┐
   │        │ Cold  │                                   /│ Peak  │\
   │        │ Open  │\                                 / │Energy │ \
   │        └───────┘ \                               /  └───────┘  \    (Button)
   │                   \  (Intro)                    /               \   ┌──────┐
   │                    \┌────────┐  (Escalation)   /                 \  │ Final│
   │                     │Baseline│ ───────────────/                   \ │ Joke │
   │                     │ Genre  │                                     \└──────┘
   │                     └────────┘                                 (Title Card)
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────► Time
```

1. **Cold Open:** 3–8 seconds. A punchy hook, intriguing visual, dynamic cinematic, or dramatic moment to stop the scroll.
2. **Intro (Grounding/Baseline):** Clearly establishes the **genre, perspective, art style, and player avatar**. Sets cognitive expectations.
3. **Escalation:** Progressively layers on mechanics, obstacles, world expansion, and higher stakes. Shows how the game evolves beyond the basic loop.
4. **Climax (Peak Energy):** Rapid, high-intensity demonstration of late-game chaos, epic boss encounters, or high-level mechanical synergy.
5. **Title Card & Call to Action (CTA):** Name of the game, platform availability, and a clear next step.
6. **The "Button" (Optional End Beat):** A 2–4 second humorous beat, narrative question mark, or surprising micro-clip after the title card to leave a lasting impression.

## 4. The 8 Common Traps in Indie Presentation & How to Fix Them

### 4.1. Mistake #1: Vague Intro & Unclear Genre
* **The Trap:** Opening with slow sweeping landscape shots, clouds, slow camera pans over empty environments, or company splash screens.
* **The Fix:** Show the player character moving/interacting immediately. If the game is a 2D precision platformer, show jumping and hazards within the first 5 seconds.

### 4.2. Mistake #2: Burying the Hook
* **The Trap:** Presenting the game as a standard entry in a genre (e.g., "It's a cozy farming sim") for 80% of the trailer before revealing the unique spin (e.g., "...with human sacrifice").
* **The Fix:** Tone and subversion must be telegraphed early (via music, audio, or visual juxtaposition).

### 4.3. Mistake #3: Flat, Looping Music
* **The Trap:** Ripping a standard 60-second in-game background track that loops without dynamics, build-up, or climactic drops.
* **The Fix:** Use or compose music structured specifically with rising intensity, hit points for key cuts, and a definite crescendo/ending.

### 4.4. Mistake #4: Features/Content Over Core Fantasy ("The Ice Cream Shop Fallacy")
* **The Trap:** Listing raw statistics: *"50+ Weapons! 16 Biomes! 4 Game Modes!"*
* **The Reality:** Derek compares this to an ice cream shop advertising that they have 30 flavors when the customer is a 30-minute drive away and doesn't even know if the ice cream is good.
* **The Fix:** Pitch the **core loop and emotion first**. Only show variety (multipliers) once the foundational appeal is proven.

### 4.5. Mistake #5: The Lore Dump
* **The Trap:** Beginning with text cards or voiceover explaining 500 years of ancient history (*"In the realm of Aethelgard, five crystals fell..."*).
* **The Fix:** Nobody cares about lore until they care about the characters and mechanics. Ground story in immediate, active conflicts happening on screen.

### 4.6. Mistake #6: Visual Overload & Clutter
* **The Trap:** Rapid cuts with no visual continuity, complex multi-layered HUDs, and disparate screen positions that disorient the viewer.
* **The Fix:** Maintain clean **Eye Trace** (leading the viewer's gaze seamlessly from shot to shot) and clear screen composition.

### 4.7. Mistake #7: Missing In-Game Sound Effects
* **The Trap:** Muting game audio and letting only the background music play.
* **The Fix:** Sound effects convey "game feel" (weight, impact, tactile responsiveness). Mixing crisp Foley/SFX with the music elevates perceived production value.

### 4.8. Mistake #8: Missing or Scattered Call to Action (CTA)
* **The Trap:** Ending without indicating where to get the game, or displaying 10 different social media/Discord links.
* **The Fix:** Provide **one unified, low-friction CTA**: e.g., *"Wishlist on Steam"* or *"Available Now on Nintendo Switch & PC"*.

## 5. Case Study Analysis & Core Directing Techniques

### 5.1. *Papers, Please* — Visual Pacing & Cognitive Bedrock
* **Technique (Linear Tutorialization):** Starts with the simplest version of the core mechanic (inspect passport $\rightarrow$ stamp $\rightarrow$ approve/deny). Once established, it escalates by introducing discrepancies, body scanners, and moral dilemmas.
* **Framing / Cropping:** Uses tight camera crops on specific UI elements (stamps, passports, rulebooks) to remove extraneous visual noise, directing the viewer’s focus exactly where critical decision-making happens.

### 5.2. *Spiritfarer* — Emotional Arc & Thematic Title Cards
* **Technique (The Narrative Hook):** Rather than generic feature cards, it uses evocative title cards specific to its theme (*"Care for your spirit friends"*, *"Learn how to say Goodbye"*).
* **Filmic Pacing:** Allows atmospheric, character-driven moments to breathe before transitioning into mechanical variety (building, cooking, platforming).

### 5.3. *Dosa Divas* — Contextualizing Abstract Mechanics
* **Before:** Showed mechanical turn-based battles and cooking in isolation, lacking a coherent narrative throughline.
* **After Consultation:** Staged an overarching narrative conflict (crushing an evil sister's industrialized fast-food empire to restore soulful homemade food). 
* **Takeaway:** Game mechanics need a clear narrative motive. Why is the player fighting? Why are they collecting?

### 5.4. *Harvest Island* — Managing Tone & Preventing Player Backlash
* **Genre Subversion Risk:** If a game looks like *Stardew Valley* but hides a dark psychological horror narrative, presenting it entirely as a cute farming sim can lead to negative reviews from players feeling deceived.
* **Solution:** Establish the baseline visual style, but use eerie, dissonant audio/music and unsettling text cards right away to signal that something is wrong under the surface.

### 5.5. *Tiny Glade* & *Celeste* — Eye Trace & Match on Action
* **Match on Action / Eye Trace:** Cutting between shots while keeping the focal point (cursor position in *Tiny Glade*, Madeline's dash trajectory in *Celeste*) in the exact same screen coordinates across camera transitions.
* **Rhythm Matching:** Aligning physics impacts and architectural drops directly with musical downbeats.

### 5.6. *Baba Is You* — Scope Shots & Multipliers
* **The Rule Tutorial:** Demonstrates rules through isolated, self-contained puzzles (*"BABA IS YOU"*, *"WALL IS STOP"*, *"FLAG IS WIN"*).
* **The Scope Shot:** Once the player understands basic syntax manipulation, cut to a wide, complex screen showing dozens of interacting rules to communicate depth and high skill ceiling.

### 5.7. *Neurocracy* & *Amarantus* — Narrative & Visual Novel Trailers
* **The Two-Times Reading Rule:** On-screen text must stay visible long enough for a viewer to read it **twice** at normal reading speed.
* **Curated Pull-Quotes:** Use reviews and dialogue snippets that explain gameplay verbs (*"A compelling exercise in collaborative online sleuthing"*) rather than empty praise (*"Great game!"*).

## 6. Developer Pipeline: Best Practices for Capture & Tooling

### 6.1. Build dedicated Capture Tools & Debug Hooks
To capture clean, cinematic gameplay footage, developers should implement internal debug tools:
* **UI / HUD Toggle:** One-button hotkey to hide/show all on-screen HUD elements.
* **Free-Cam / Cinematic Camera Rig:** Ability to detach the player camera for smooth pans, wide shots, and dramatic angles.
* **Game Speed Modifiers:** Sliders or hotkeys for $0.5\times$, $0.25\times$, and paused states to capture frame-perfect action.
* **State / Inventory Injectors:** Shortcuts to instantly equip specific weapons, spawn specific enemies, or trigger set pieces without manual playthroughs.

### 6.2. Production Timelines & Resourcing
* **Lead Time:** Professional trailer production requires **4 to 6 weeks** from brief/concepting through draft revisions, capture, and audio mastering.
* **Opportunity Cost for Solo/Small Devs:** If a programmer or artist spends 4–6 weeks learning editing software and cutting trailers, calculate the development delay versus hiring specialized external help.

## Summary Checklist for Indie Game Pitches & Trailers

1. [ ] **First 5 Seconds:** Is the core genre, character, and primary mechanic instantly recognizable?
2. [ ] **Hook Telegraphed:** Is the unique selling proposition (USP) shown early rather than saved as an unprompted final twist?
3. [ ] **Dynamic Audio:** Does the music have distinct rise, escalation, and impact points matching the visual cuts?
4. [ ] **Game SFX Integrated:** Are impacts, footsteps, UI sounds, and abilities audible alongside the soundtrack?
5. [ ] **Eye-Trace Maintained:** Do camera transitions follow natural focal points across consecutive shots?
6. [ ] **Concise Text:** Can all on-screen title cards and dialogue prompts be comfortably read twice?
7. [ ] **Multipliers in Context:** Are biomes, weapons, and modes shown as extensions of the core loop rather than bulleted lists?
8. [ ] **Single Clear CTA:** Does the final screen tell the player exactly what one action to take next (e.g., Wishlist on Steam)?

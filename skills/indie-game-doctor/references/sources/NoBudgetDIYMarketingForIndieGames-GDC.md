# Comprehensive Knowledge Base: Indie Game Direction, Production & Marketing Strategy
*(Synthesized from Michelle Lega, Senior Producer at Armor Games Studios — GDC Session)*

---

## 1. Game Concept, Positioning & Vision Communication

### A. The Core Message (The "Elevator Pitch")
Marketing and game direction intersect at the **core message**: if the development team cannot succinctly articulate what the game is, players and press will not understand it either.

* **Clarity Over Lore:** Strip away superfluous world-building lore in initial positioning. Highlight the core verbs, player dynamic, and setting hook.
* **Avoid Jargon & Clunky Comparisons:** Do not rely on comparisons to obscure games (e.g., calling a game "Mafia in space" is less descriptive than defining the actual social interaction: *"a party game of teamwork and betrayal for 4–15 players... in space"* as seen in *Among Us*).

#### Three Standard Pitch Formulas
1. **Action / Verb Focus (for mechanics-heavy or arcade games):**
   $$\text{"[Verb] in a [noun] while you [verb]."}$$
   * *Example:* *"Lasso evil horses in the Wild West while competing for the big roller derby title."*
2. **Genre & Core Hook (for genre-enthusiast / systemic games):**
   $$\text{"[Game Name] is a [genre] where you [verb] and [verb]."}$$
   * *Example:* *"Horse Bandits is a roguelike shoot-'em-up where you shoot horseshoes and lassos at horses to stop them from stomping your crops."*
3. **Narrative / Call-to-Action (for story-driven / adventure games):**
   $$\text{"[Story Details]. Will you [verb]?"}$$
   * *Example:* *"[Story setting & crisis]. Will you take up the lasso?"* (e.g., *Alba: A Wildlife Adventure* introduces Alba saving her island wildlife and starting a revolution).

---

### B. Defining the 6–7 Core Unique Feature Bullet Points
Bullet points serve as the backbone for Steam store pages, press kits, and publisher pitches. They must highlight what makes the title distinct, not generic genre tropes.

* **Visual & Audio Identity:** Specify the pixel art fidelity, 2D/3D presentation, soundtrack composers, or distinct aesthetic inspirations (e.g., *Shovel Knight*'s 8-bit homage and Jake Kaufman soundtrack).
* **Core Gameplay Loops & Mechanics:** Highlight tactile actions (dueling, jumping, digging, upgrading).
* **Game Modes & Accessibility:** Detail campaign length, single-player vs. co-op/multiplayer capacity, challenge modes, and accessibility considerations.

## 2. Audience Identification & Alignment

### A. Non-Obvious Target Audiences
Indie games should not be positioned for "all gamers." Define specific subsets:
* **Genre Fans:** Target sub-genres directly (e.g., Roguelike, Cozy Sim, Soulslike, Precision Platformer).
* **Mechanic Lovers:** Players obsessed with specific tactile mechanics (e.g., movement tech, grappling hooks, inventory management).
* **Narrative/Thematic Enthusiasts:** Players who enjoy specific thematic tones (whimsical, horror, ecological preservation).
* **Adjacent Non-Gaming Interests:** Look for real-world hobbies and cultural groups matching game themes:
  * *Horse game* $\rightarrow$ Equestrian communities, Western film fans.
  * *Space Otter Charlie* $\rightarrow$ Reached out to the **Monterey Bay Aquarium**, which streamed the game on Twitch to animal lovers.

## 3. Community Building & Psychology: "People Follow People"

### A. The Core Principle
In the indie ecosystem, players connect to **human creators**, not faceless corporate entities.
* **Authenticity & Humanity:** Share developer struggles, funny bugs, behind-the-scenes milestones, and personal perspectives.
* **Tone Harmonization:** If making a serious, dark, or atmospheric game, developers do **not** need to maintain an in-character brooding persona online. Gamers appreciate the contrast of a fun, warm creator building a dark world.
* **Zero Drama Policy:** Avoid public feuds, harsh critiques of other developers, or cynical engagement bait. Drama attracts toxic followings rather than long-term supporters.

```
└───────────────────────┘        └───────────────────────┘       └───────────────────────┘
```

### 1. Steam Page
* **Launch Early:** Set up the page as soon as there are solid key art assets, clear copy, and representative screenshots/GIFs (art can be updated later).
* **Conversion Expectation:** As a general baseline, expect **10%–30% of total pre-launch wishlists** to convert to sales within the first year.
* **Maintenance:** Utilize Steam Announcements, participate in seasonal Steam events/Next Fests, optimize search tags, and actively monitor Community Hub discussion boards.

### 2. Website & Press Kit
* Maintain a single, clean press kit link (e.g., using *presskit()* or similar tools).
* Include: high-res key art (both with logo and logo-free for editorial use), 6–10 curated gameplay screenshots, trailer download links, developer bios, release dates/platforms, and clear contact emails.

### 3. TikTok & Vertical Video (9:16)
* The most effective modern channel for organic viral reach and wishlist spikes.
* **Formats that work:**
  * Before-and-after development evolution clips.
  * Hilarious physics glitches / bug showcases.
  * Solo-dev relatable storytelling and face-to-camera explanations.
* Leverage trending audio tracks, niche game hashtags (`#indiedev`, `#gamedev`), and short, punchy gameplay demonstrations.

### 4. Discord Community Management
* **Role:** High-touch retention and community cultivation (not a replacement for broad social reach).
* **Best Practices:** Establish clear server rules, conduct community polls on minor design details, host developer streams/voice chats, and reward members with exclusive beta builds or playtests.

## 5. The "1 Hour a Day" Indie Marketing System

For solo developers and small teams with zero budget, allocate **5 hours per week (1 hour/day)** to build sustained momentum without interrupting core production:

| Day | Focus Area | Specific Tasks & Execution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monday** | **Social Media Scheduling & Community Engagement** | • Schedule weekly native posts across Twitter/X, TikTok, Instagram.<br>• Reply to community comments, check direct messages, engage with peer developers' posts. |
| **Tuesday** | **Press List & Streamer Outreach Building** | • Maintain and expand a spreadsheet of journalists, niche review sites, and content creators.<br>• Track: Name, Email, Twitter/Social, Platforms covered, Content format, Region, and Notes on their favorite games. |
| **Wednesday** | **Networking & Showcase/Festival Submissions** | • Participate in local indie developer meetups and online developer Discords.<br>• Research and submit builds to upcoming festivals and awards (e.g., Steam Next Fest, Day of the Devs, PAX, IndieCade, IGF) via aggregators like *The Indie Houses*. |
| **Thursday** | **Devlogs & Steam Maintenance** | • Draft short devlog updates detailing new features, fixes, or behind-the-scenes insights.<br>• Cross-post devlogs to Steam Announcements, Discord, and personal website.<br>• Update Steam copy, tags, and review community discussion forums. |
| **Friday** | **Content Capture & Asset Preparation** | • Capture raw gameplay clips, high-resolution screenshots, and GIFs.<br>• Edit vertical (9:16) and widescreen (16:9) video cuts.<br>• Organize assets into dedicated folders (`/ToPost`, `/AlreadyPosted`) for Monday's queue. |

## 6. Key Production Beats & Launch Lifecycle

```
[ Announcement ] ──► [ Launch Window ] ──► [ Demos / Festivals ] ──► [ Exact Date (1-3 Mo.) ] ──► [ Launch Day ] ──► [ Post-Launch ]
```

1. **Game Announcement:** Unveil trailer, key art, and launch the Steam page simultaneously. Send out targeted press emails with direct Steam keys or demo builds.
2. **Launch Timeframe Announcement:** Announce seasonal window (e.g., "Coming Q3 2024").
3. **Demo Drops & Festival Cycles:** Participate in Steam Next Fest or release a standalone demo on Itch.io. Pitch to major showcase events (PAX, MIX, Day of the Devs).
4. **Exact Launch Date Announcement:** Lock and announce the exact date **1 to 3 months prior to release**. Sending review codes 2–4 weeks early with a strict review embargo date.
5. **Launch Day Protocol:**
   * Distribute game release press release via distribution hubs (e.g., *gamespress.com*) and email lists.
   * Send Steam keys directly to press/streamers (eliminate friction—do not make them request keys).
   * Launch with a **Launch Discount** (standard industry practice).
   * Politely ask community and peers for honest Steam reviews (crucial for Steam algorithm visibility).
6. **Post-Launch & Long-Tail Strategy:**
   * **Re-Announcements:** Treat every major update, DLC, port (console/mobile), localization release, and seasonal discount as a new marketing beat.
   * **Patch Notes & Transparency:** Respond promptly and empathetically to bug reports in public reviews.

---

## 7. Operational & Production Guidelines

* **When to Begin Marketing:** Begin baseline awareness as soon as the project is publicly announced; ramp up active cycles **6 to 12 months before launch**.
* **Organic vs. Paid Promotion:** For budget-constrained indies, paid social ads and promoted posts generally provide poor conversion ROI. Prioritize organic, authentic content and press/creator outreach.
* **Protecting Game Value:** Distribute keys generously to verified press, creators, and close community testers, but avoid mass unmonitored giveaways to prevent devaluing the product.
* **Redefining Success Beyond Metrics:** Avoid judging project success solely against viral outlier titles. Valid metrics include finishing and shipping a polished title, gaining a core cohort of passionate fans, positive critical feedback, or achieving sustainable milestone sales.
